The gradient tells us which direction to move: for cat, it’s the direction of meows. But it doesn’t tell us how far. If we added the whole gradient, cat would jump much too far and could overshoot. So we multiply the gradient by the learning rate η, a small number like 0.1. That shrinks the arrow to a small step, η∇s. The faint blue arrow is the full gradient and the solid blue arrow is the small step. To update, we add that step to cat: x₁ becomes x₁ + ηx₂ and y₁ becomes y₁ + ηy₂. The new cat points a bit more toward meows, so their dot product goes up. For a pair we want to push apart, like student and meows, we subtract the step instead. Training repeats this tiny step thousands of times, and that’s the slow movement you see in the demo.
