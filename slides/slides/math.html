Call cat (x₁, y₁) and meows (x₂, y₂). The score s is their dot product: multiply matching parts and add, x₁x₂ plus y₁y₂. A partial derivative asks: if I nudge only one number, how fast does s change? If x₁ goes up by 1, the x₁x₂ part goes up by x₂ and the y₁y₂ part doesn’t change at all. So the partial derivative of s with respect to x₁ is x₂. The same reasoning gives y₂ for y₁. Stack the two partial derivatives into one vector and you get the gradient: (x₂, y₂), which is exactly the meows vector. The picture shows it: the blue gradient arrow from the tip of cat points the same way meows does. So to make the score bigger, move cat a little in the direction of meows. To make it smaller, as for student and meows, move the other way.
